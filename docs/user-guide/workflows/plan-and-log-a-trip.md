# Workflow: plan a trip, run it, and write it up

🇬🇧 **English** · 🇷🇴 [Română](../ro/workflows/plan-and-log-a-trip.md)

[← Workflows](README.md) · Reference: [Trips](../features/trips.md) ·
[Checklists and the callout](../features/checklists-and-callout.md)

---

A trip in SilexGIS covers the whole life of the thing: an idea somebody floats, a plan people
answer, a party underground with a callout time, and a report that writes itself up
afterwards. This walkthrough follows all of it.

---

## Stage 1 — Float the idea

**Trip logs → New trip log.**

Give it a title, a **trip purpose** (exploration, survey, maintenance, training, tourism,
rescue, science, other — plus whatever your club has added), and dates. A trip is logged over
the **days it actually ran**, not a single date.

Set its **visibility** — this is who may read the trip at all. And note the banner: *"This is
a draft. Nobody has been notified about it yet."* Drafts are for writing; nothing goes out.

Then **Float it** to propose it, or go straight to **Start organising**.

## Stage 2 — Plan it

The trip's **Plan** card holds what a party settles before it sets off. Which fields appear
depends on the trip's purpose — a club describes the form once and can change it later
without invalidating reports already written.

Typically:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Meeting** | Meeting time, meeting point described in words |
| **Transport** | Drivers, seats available, departure points |
| **Gear** | Equipment and rigging notes |
| **Permits** | Permit required? Permit obtained? Permit reference, permit holder, key holder, landowner contact, access notes |
| **Weather** | Weather note |
| **Talking** | Group chat link |
| **Safety** | Callout contact |

### The meeting point on the map

You can draw **where the party gathers** — a point, or the walk in to it — on the same kind
of map as the trip's own sketch.

> ⚠ **Read the warning beside it.** A meeting point is shown **exactly as placed** to
> everybody who may read the trip. It is never approximated. A meeting point dropped by a
> guarded entrance places that entrance for everybody invited.

### The checklist

If the trip's purpose points at a [checklist](../features/checklists-and-callout.md), the
trip's page shows it as lines to tick off, with a quiet *3 of 7* on the trip listings.
Ticking records who said so and when.

**Nothing is ever refused because a list is unfinished.** It tells the party where they are;
it does not stand in their way, and it decides nothing about who may read the plan.

### The callout

**Arrange a callout**: record when the party expects to be out, and the hour to raise the
alarm if nobody has said so.

If that hour passes with nothing said, everyone the trip concerns who may read it gets an
email. It names the trip, its date and the expected hour — and **never a cave**, because an
email is as much a copy of a protected location as anything on a screen.

Anybody the trip names or has asked can **stand it down** in one tap, whether or not they may
edit the record — because the person who knows the party is out is the one on the trip.

A trip with an armed alarm also shows **when the check last ran**. Silence is the good news
in a callout, so a watcher that has stopped and a party safely underground look identical;
the page comes down on the side of telling you nobody has looked.

## Stage 3 — Ask people

The **Who is coming** tab.

**Ask** somebody from the club's roster. An invitation grants nothing — see the box below.

Each person answers for themselves: **coming, not coming, maybe**, with a line of their own
beside it. Somebody who may correct the trip can answer on behalf of a person who phoned in.

If the trip sets **Places**, the list says how many are taken and how many are waiting, **in
the order people said yes**. First come; a change of mind goes to the back. The organiser can
still **pick** somebody further down the queue, and that pick does not outlive the answer it
was made about.

A limit never refuses an answer. It only decides where in the queue an answer sits.

> ### An invitation grants nothing
>
> Being asked onto a trip does not open a cave. When somebody asked onto a trip cannot read a
> cave the trip is about, the **cave's owner and the full administrators** get a message with
> a link to it.
>
> That message is honest about its own reach: it says it went to the owner and the
> administrators and nobody else, that somebody who could grant access another way — through
> a club, say — has **not** been told, and asks the reader to pass it on if it is not theirs
> to act on. Somebody on the list with no account gets nothing and can be granted nothing.

## Stage 4 — Run it

**It is going ahead** confirms the trip. If plans change: **Put it back** (delayed), then
**Settle a new date**, or **Call it off** — which keeps the trip, says so on every listing,
and stops both the callout alarm and the coming-up reminder.

The people on a trip get an email when it is coming up, once.

## Stage 5 — Write it up

Afterwards, **Mark as done**, then fill in the record.

### Who was there

The roster. Each person carries **what they did** — leader, driver, surveyor, photographer,
trainee, instructor, callout contact, participant, proposer, plus whatever your club has
added. Somebody who did two jobs is recorded doing both.

Each person can also carry **their own times** (for the one who came out early) and **a line
of their own** — *turned back at the pitch head* — so nobody has to invent a job to record a
circumstance.

If people answered before the trip, one deliberate act — **Write these people onto the
trip** — turns everybody holding a place into people who were there. It tells nobody:
everybody it writes in asked to be there and was told when they were asked.

### What the trip did to what it names

This is the **What this trip did** section, and it is the *only* way a trip is tied to a
place. There is no separate cave list to keep in step.

Each is a labelled field you fill in as the trip earns it:

**Work areas · Main work features · Visited · Surveyed · Discovered · Dug · Photographed ·
Searched, not found · Leads left · Follows on from**

A cave's own page returns the favour: its **Trips** section lists the trips that named it,
newest first.

> A trip **names only the caves its reader is allowed to open**, and tells them how many it
> is not naming rather than leaving a list quietly short — on the page, in the report and in
> the generated document.

### The figures

Depth reached, length surveyed, survey stations, rope used — in metres, once, shown in your
own language's numbers. Plus underground time, worked out over however many days the trip
really ran.

### The report sections

Driven by the trip's purpose: **Field data** (conditions, water level, objective reached,
leads found and remaining, survey grade, instrument, loops closed, sketch completed, new
passage), **Logistics** (cost and what it covered, access notes), and **Safety**.

### Incidents

**Something went wrong** is a flag anyone who may read the trip can see and search for. The
account of *what* went wrong — what happened, severity (near miss / minor / serious /
rescue), equipment failure, lessons learned, who it was reported to — is shown **only to the
people who may correct the trip**. A near-miss names somebody's mistake, and it is not gossip
for the whole club.

### The trip's sketch

A point, a line or an area for where the trip happened.

> ⚠ Like the meeting point, a trip sketch is shown **exactly as drawn** to everyone who may
> read the trip, whatever protection the caves it names carry.

## Stage 6 — Publish

**Publish** announces the trip to the people named on it — and only to those of them who may
actually open it. The confirmation tells you the audience in plain words before you commit,
and says that it cannot be taken back.

## Stage 7 — The write-up

**Report view** lays the whole trip out as a document, with a print stylesheet, and offers a
**Word document** you can download or **file against the trip itself**.

The write-up is built from **your** reading of the trip and nothing else — no cave you may
not place, no account of an incident if you are only a reader, and photographs go in as the
same renderings the gallery shows you, with camera metadata stripped.

A copy filed against the trip is narrower still: it is written for whoever may read the trip
*at all*, not for the person who filed it.

There is no map picture in it — the sketch is written out in words — and there is no public
address for a report. It is downloaded by somebody signed in who may read the trip.

Your club can write **its own layout**: download the standard one (a short, self-explaining
text file), edit it, upload it under Configuration → Report layouts, and choose it. A layout
can only ask for things the reader was already given, and a line whose contents turn out
empty simply disappears.

## Afterwards

- **Photographs** — the trip has a gallery and a headline picture, and photographs filed
  against it can [become places on the map](photographs-to-places.md).
- **Your own trips** — *Trip logs → My trips* answers *what am I going on, and when*:
  soonest first, including trips you have been asked about and not turned down. It is worked
  out from whoever is signed in and cannot be asked about anybody else.
- **Statistics** — a person, a cave and a club each get their totals, counted over the trips
  you may read. See [Measurements and statistics](../features/measurements-and-statistics.md).
