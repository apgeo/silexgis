A trip covers the whole life of the thing: an idea, a plan people answer, a party underground
with a callout, and a report. This page is the reference; the
[workflow](../workflows/plan-and-log-a-trip.md) is the narrative.

---

## The trip record

A trip is logged **over the days it actually ran**, not on a single date.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Title** · **Trip purpose** · **Dates** · **Location** | |
| **Organizing caving group** | |
| **Entry / exit time** and **underground time** | Worked out over however many days it ran |
| **Visibility** | Who may read the trip at all |
| **Places** | How many people it has room for. Empty means no limit |
| **State** | See below |

### Trip purposes

Ships with **Exploration · Survey / mapping · Maintenance / rigging · Training ·
Tourism / visit · Rescue · Science · Other**. These cannot be renamed out from under the
software — but a club that runs something nobody thought of **adds it themselves**, and it is
filterable and countable from that moment.

Each purpose also decides **what a report of that kind asks for** — the three report sections
below — and can point at a [checklist](checklists-and-callout.md).

## States

`draft → proposed → planned → confirmed → done → published`, with `cancelled` and `delayed`.

| Action | |
|---|---|
| **Float it** | Proposed — an idea |
| **Start organising** | Planned |
| **It is going ahead** | Confirmed |
| **Put it back** | Delayed |
| **Settle a new date** | Back to being organised |
| **Mark as done** | |
| **Publish** | Announces it to the people named on it |
| **Call it off** | Kept, and reinstatable as a draft |
| **Back to draft** | Unpublish |

**A draft tells nobody.** You can write up over several sittings with nothing going out.
Publishing is what announces it — and only to the people who may actually open it. The
confirmation names the audience in plain words and says it cannot be taken back.

A draft is **not a hidden trip**: whoever the trip's visibility admits can read it from the
moment it exists.

## Who is coming (before)

The invitation list. **Ask** somebody the club knows — a person is asked by their entry in
the roster.

Each answers for themselves: **Coming · Not coming · Maybe · Not answered**, with a note.
Somebody who may correct the trip may answer on behalf of a person who phoned in.

With **Places** set: *"n of m places taken, k waiting"* — **in the order people answered**.
First come; a change of mind goes to the back. The organiser can **Pick** somebody further
down, and that pick does not outlive the answer it was made about.

Without: *"n coming. This trip sets no limit on how many."*

**A limit never refuses an answer.** It only decides where in the queue an answer sits.

Somebody on the list you may not be told about is **counted, not named**: *"n on the list,
not shown to you."*

### Turning answers into a roster

**Write these people onto the trip** turns everybody holding a place into people who were
there. It **tells nobody** — everybody it writes in asked to be there and was told when they
were asked. It is refused before the trip has happened.

### An invitation grants nothing

When somebody asked onto a trip cannot read a cave the trip is about, the **cave's owner and
the full administrators** get a message, whether the cave was already on the trip or added
afterwards.

That message says it went to the owner and the administrators and **nobody else**, that
somebody who could grant access another way has **not** been told, and asks the reader to
pass it on if it is not theirs to act on. Somebody with no account gets nothing and can be
granted nothing.

## Participants (after)

Who was there, and **what they did**: Participant · Proposer · Leader · Driver · Surveyor ·
Photographer · Trainee · Instructor · Callout contact — from a list your club owns and
extends. *Attending* and *proposing* are there from the start and cannot be removed.

Somebody who did two jobs is **recorded doing both** rather than made to choose.

Each person can carry **their own entry and exit times** (left blank, the trip's own apply)
and **a note of their own** — *turned back at the pitch head* — so nobody has to invent a job
to record a circumstance.

> That detail is part of the trip and **goes no further than the trip does**. You cannot
> search for the trips somebody led: that is a question about a person, assembled out of
> records the asker may never be allowed to read.

## What this trip did

The **only** way a trip is tied to a place. There is no separate cave list to keep in step.

**Work areas · Main work features · Visited · Surveyed · Discovered · Dug · Photographed ·
Searched, not found · Leads left · Follows on from**

Each is a labelled field you fill in as the trip earns it, rather than a form of empty boxes
to stare at. A [work area](work-areas.md) shows what contains it, so two passages of the same
name are told apart. A lead can carry a description of what was left.

What one person records, everybody who may maintain the thing it is about can correct.

A cave's own page returns the favour with a **Trips** section listing the trips that named
it.

> **A trip names only the caves its reader is allowed to open**, and tells them how many it is
> not naming — *"n not shown to you"* — on the page, in the report and in the generated
> document. Naming a cave, even by its bare identifier, is as good as showing it to somebody
> a trip invited in.

## The report sections

Driven by the trip's purpose — a form the club describes once and can change later **without
invalidating a single report already written**.

**Measured** — Depth reached · Length surveyed · Survey stations · Rope used. In metres, once,
shown in your own language's numbers.

**Field data** — Conditions underground · Water level (low / normal / high / in flood) ·
Objective reached · Leads found · Leads still open · Survey grade (1–6, X) · Instrument ·
Loops closed · Sketch completed · New passage.

**Logistics** — Permit reference, permit holder, key holder, landowner contact, access notes,
cost and what it covered.

**Safety** — see below.

Plus free text: **Results / observations** and **Weather / conditions**.

### Incidents

**Something went wrong** is a flag anyone who may read the trip can see and search for.

The **account** of what went wrong — what happened, severity (near miss / minor / serious /
rescue), equipment failure, lessons learned, who it was reported to — is shown **only to the
people who may correct the trip**. Everyone else sees *"Not shown to you"* with the reason: a
near-miss names somebody's mistake, and it is not gossip for the whole club.

## The plan

The same card, for what a party settles before it sets off: meeting time and described
meeting point, drivers, seats, departure points, equipment and rigging, whether a permit is
required and obtained, the forecast, the group chat link, and the callout contact.

### The meeting point on the map

A point, or the walk in to it, drawn on the same kind of map as the trip's sketch.

> ⚠ **Exact position — where the party gathers is never approximated.** Everyone who may read
> the trip sees it exactly as placed. A meeting point near a guarded entrance places that
> entrance for everybody invited.

### The trip sketch

A point, a line or an area for where the trip happened. Pick a shape, then draw; a new shape
replaces the one before it.

> ⚠ Same warning. **A trip's sketch is never approximated** — it is shown exactly as drawn to
> everyone who may read the trip, *whatever protection the caves the trip names carry*.

## The write-up

**Report view** lays the trip out as a document with a print stylesheet, and produces a
**Word document** to download or **file against the trip**.

- Built from **your** reading of the trip and nothing else. It can never contain something the
  page would not have shown you.
- Photographs go in as the same renderings the gallery shows you, **camera metadata stripped**.
- **A copy filed against the trip is narrower still** — written for whoever may read the trip
  at all, not for the person who filed it.
- **No map picture** — the sketch is written out in words.
- **No public address.** A report is downloaded by somebody signed in who may read the trip.

Your club can write **its own layout** — download the standard one (a short, self-explaining
text file), edit, upload under Configuration → Report layouts, choose it. A layout can only
ask for things the reader was already given, and a line whose contents turn out empty simply
disappears, so the same layout produces an honest document for a member and for an editor.

## Importing a club spreadsheet

**Trip logs → Import** takes the *centralizator* a club has kept for years — one row per trip,
in a spreadsheet — and turns it into trips. It is one screen, and nothing is written until you
press the button at the bottom.

Upload the sheet and the screen fills in with what it thinks the file says. Above the table are
the choices that apply to the whole file:

- **Which column is which.** It guesses from the headers and you correct it from a list of the
  sheet's own header names.
- **Day first or month first.** Ambiguous dates are the thing that silently moves a year of
  trips by six months, so the screen says what it worked the order out from and how many rows
  depend on the answer.
- **What separates several things in one cell** — the participants, the caves.
- **What may be created.** Caves, areas, people and trip types the sheet names but the
  installation does not have. Each is a separate switch and **every one starts off**, so an
  import adds nothing to a register unless you ask it to. You can only turn on a switch for a
  register you are allowed to add to anyway.
- **Who will be able to see the trips, and which club they belong to.**

Below that is one row per line of the sheet, with what it matched, and beside it everything the
file could not be read cleanly. Any row can be set aside. Anything the reader could not make
sense of at all is listed as a problem and is never imported.

### The counts, and the one that matters

Four figures say what confirming would add — trips, caves, areas, people. Beside them are the
figures that say **what needs you**: names more than one person on the roster answers to, and
names that cannot become a person at all.

That second kind is the one to read carefully. A cell holding `Ion A.` or a single first name
cannot be turned into a roster entry — there is no way to tell it apart from the next person of
the same name later on — so **those people will not be listed on their trips**. The screen says
so plainly, above the button, before you press it. Their names are not thrown away: they are
kept in the trip's own text. If that matters to you, fix those cells in the spreadsheet before
importing.

An ambiguous name is different: you settle it on the screen by picking which person is meant.

### After

Confirming files the whole thing as one batch. Imported trips are **silent** — nobody is
notified, nobody is invited — and they land finished rather than as drafts.

The batch appears under **Geodata → Imports**, where it can be undone. Undo removes the trips.
It does **not** take back cavers or trip-type words the import created, because a word other
trips may since have been given is not this batch's to remove.

## My trips

**Trip logs → My trips** answers *what am I going on, and when*: the trips you are named on,
plus the ones you have been asked about and not turned down, **ordered by when they happen**,
nearest at the top. The dashboard carries the next few.

- A **called-off** trip stays on the list and says so, because that is the thing you most need
  to notice.
- Narrow to one state, or to a range of dates.
- It is worked out from whoever is signed in and **cannot be asked about anybody else** —
  even the number of rows would say where that person has been.
