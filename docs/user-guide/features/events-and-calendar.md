# Events and calendar

[← Feature reference](README.md) · Related: [Trips](trips.md) · [Camps](camps.md)

---

Two things: **Events** are the club's own dates that are not trips or camps, and the
**Calendar** is one window over everything dated.

---

# Events

**Activity → Events.**

A meeting, a training session, a maintenance day, a gear check, a conference or a deadline.

| Field | |
|---|---|
| **Title** · **Description** · **Place** | The place is written as an address |
| **Kind** | Club meeting · Training · Working day · Gear check · Conference · Deadline |
| **Dates** | A day, and optionally a second day it runs on to |
| **Time** | Optional start and finish times |
| **Caving group** | |
| **Places** | How many people it has room for |
| **State** | |

## Times are the club's wall clock

**A 19:00 meeting reads as 19:00 to everybody, wherever they happen to be.** That is what a
club that meets in one place means by seven in the evening, and it is deliberately not
converted into each reader's zone.

## Who may read one

The same way as everything else: yours alone unless you say otherwise, your club's if you are
in one, and anybody you name individually through the same sharing dialog every other record
uses.

## States

An event goes through the same stages a trip or a camp does:

**Float the idea → Start organising → It is going ahead → It happened**, with **Put it back**
(and then **Settle a new date**) and **Call it off**. **Announce** is the event's equivalent
of publishing; **Back to draft** un-announces.

## Answering an event

**An event is answered the way a trip is.** Somebody is asked, each person says yes, no or
maybe, and if the event sets a number of places the answers decide who is in and who is
waiting — the same list, in the order people answered, with the organiser free to pick
somebody out of turn. A limit never refuses an answer.

Two differences from a trip:

- **A deadline takes no answers at all** — a date to have something in by is not something to
  sign up for — so it is offered no list. (Changing an event's kind to *deadline* is refused
  once people have answered.)
- **An event keeps no separate record of who turned up.** The answers *are* the record, so
  there is nothing to turn into anything.

## Events that come round again

**It comes round again** writes the whole run now, as ordinary events. Each one is then
edited, answered and called off on its own.

- **How often**: every day · every week · every other week · every month.
- **In your own words**: how you would describe the repetition to somebody reading the
  calendar — *"every Tuesday"*.
- **How many**, or **up to and including** a last day, or both. A repetition told neither is
  refused — nothing is written unbounded, and there is a cap on how far ahead a run may reach.

An occurrence shows a banner saying it is one of a run, its described rule, and a link to
**see the other occurrences**.

Editing one asks **This change applies to**: *only this occurrence*, or *this one and every
later one*. If the run holds an occurrence that is not yours to change, **none of it is
changed** rather than part of it.

Calling off the rest of a run keeps occurrences that have already begun, whatever you choose:
each is the record of something that happened.

# The calendar

**Activity → Calendar.** One window over **trips, camps and events** read together.

It reads as a **month, a year, a week or an agenda**, over a map.

You can:

- sort it,
- narrow to one kind,
- narrow to a club's own,
- narrow to your own,
- show what is still to come and what already happened,
- switch off called-off entries (they are marked, not hidden by default),
- see a **postponed** entry with a postponed mark rather than pretending the date it still
  carries is one anybody is going on.

## What the calendar will not tell you

> **A calendar row never names a cave** — not the cave, not a count of caves left out. A
> glance at a month can never place a protected entrance.

The title is shown as the person wrote it. You open the trip itself to see where it went.

A trip nobody has been shown yet (a draft) is **not drawn** on the calendar — but that is only
about drawing. It stays exactly as readable as it was, on its own page and on the trip lists,
to everybody who could read it before.

## Bounds

You have to say which days you want, and the span is capped. If a window holds more than the
page will return, it tells you **how many rows it left out** rather than quietly showing you
less than there is.
