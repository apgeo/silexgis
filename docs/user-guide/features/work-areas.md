**Work areas** sits at the top level of the rail, beside the map — because it is a way of
looking at the ground, not a register of what is in it, and it is where most readers start a
season.

---

## What a work area is

The ground your club actually works: a system, a sector, a passage, a dig. It is drawn as an
area on the map and recorded as a work area.

Work areas **nest**. An area shows **what contains it**, which is what tells two passages of
the same name apart — *"Sump Passage"* in one system is not *"Sump Passage"* in another when
each is shown under its parent.

## Creating one

Draw an area on the map and record it as a work area. If none exist yet, the page says so:
*"No work area has been marked yet. Draw an area on the map and record it as a work area."*

## The page

- **All areas** / **At this level** — browse the whole set or just the current level.
- Each area lists **areas inside** it, with a count, or *"Nothing inside this one yet."*
- **On the map** opens it in the map workspace.
- **See all**.

If an installation holds more work areas than the view lists, it says so:
*"Not every work area is shown."*

## Where work areas are used

Work areas are the first of a trip's **[What this trip did](trips.md#what-this-trip-did)**
fields: *Work areas* — the ground a trip worked in.

That is what makes a season legible afterwards: which areas were touched, how often, and by
whom — as far as the reader is allowed to know.
